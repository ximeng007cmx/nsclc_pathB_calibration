Dear Editor,

We submit for your consideration an Original Research article entitled **"Immune and stromal content does not explain the effect sizes of published inflammation- and metabolism-related prognostic signatures in non-small cell lung cancer: a calibration study of 49 signatures with positive and negative controls"** (Mingxiang Chen).

**What the study asks.** Hundreds of transcriptomic prognostic signatures have been published for non-small cell lung cancer, many of them anchored to inflammation or to metabolic reprogramming. Because signature scores correlate strongly with tumour microenvironment (TME) content, it is widely assumed that TME content inflates their apparent prognostic effect and that adjusting for it should dissolve that effect. That assumption has not been tested systematically, and it cannot be tested without a positive control: an adjustment that has no power will produce a null result indistinguishable from a genuine one.

**What we did.** We assembled 49 published inflammation-related (n = 11), metabolism-related (n = 32) and immune-related (n = 6) signatures under rules fixed before analysis and hash-locked, and quantified attenuation as A = 1 − β<sub>M3</sub>/β<sub>M1</sub> in TCGA-LUAD (482 patients, 177 deaths). The design surrounds that estimate with a positive control axis (lymphocyte/cytotoxic, a known immune proxy), two negative control axes, a per-signature random-panel null (200 draws), two independent cohorts (GSE68465, GSE31210), two alternative endpoints (PFI, DSS) and a histology check in TCGA-LUSC.

**What we found.** Median attenuation across the 49 signatures was −1.4% (95% CI −7.4% to +1.8%), with a direction split of 21 shrinking, 25 growing and 3 reversing, and no class-wide loss of significance (31 significant before adjustment, 31 after). The same adjustment abolished the lymphocyte control axis (68.9% attenuation, P 0.053 to 0.775), so the null result is not a power failure. Published signatures moved less under adjustment than randomly drawn gene panels of matched size did (median percentile 0.440; Kolmogorov–Smirnov D = 0.267, P = 0.001). Attenuation magnitude tracked the signature–ImmuneScore correlation (partial ρ = 0.373, P = 0.009) while its sign did not (ρ = −0.125, P = 0.391). The practical implication is a reporting one: signature studies should report unadjusted and TME-adjusted coefficients together, and state the direction of change, rather than treat TME content as a confounder presumed to inflate their results.

**Why Cancer Medicine.** The paper addresses a methodological question that bears directly on how prognostic biomarker studies in lung cancer are reported and read. It is a calibration and reproducibility study rather than a new signature, and we believe its readership, which includes both clinical oncology and cancer bioinformatics, is the right audience for a result that qualifies current practice across a large published literature.

**Transparency measures.** Inclusion rules were pre-registered and then amended eight times, with each amendment, registry and deliverable recorded under SHA-256 in an append-only ledger. No signature was added or removed on the basis of its result. Post hoc components (waves 3 and 4, external replication, endpoint sensitivity, histology check, the ten-tool cross-check) are labelled as such in the text. Every number in the manuscript is generated by scripts that read the result tables directly, and a reconciliation script re-checks all 146 reported values against those tables; all 58 references were verified against Europe PMC and CrossRef. Two limitations are stated explicitly rather than repaired after the fact: one inflammation-specific query returned 788 hits of which the API exposed only the first 500, and 48 records that reached full-text extraction could not be converted into a scoreable signature.

**Declarations.** The manuscript is original, has not been published previously in whole or in part, and is not under consideration elsewhere. The author has approved the submitted version and agrees to be accountable for the work. The author declares no competing interests. The study used only publicly available, de-identified data (TCGA, GEO/ArrayExpress, PubMed Central full texts), so no ethics approval or informed consent was required. All code, hash-locked registries, result tables and the calibrated expression matrix are openly available (Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23537008; code repository https://github.com/ximeng007cmx/nsclc_pathB_calibration). The manuscript contains 4 figures, 3 tables, an abstract of 318 words and approximately 4,100 words of main text.

We hope the manuscript is suitable for peer review and look forward to your response.
